# Review of the AGI Design Checklist

> **Provenance:** Generated by Claude (Anthropic) from prompts written by David W. Thomas. The text is Claude output, not original writing by David W. Thomas. It is a commentary on [`AGIDesignChecklist.md`](AGIDesignChecklist.md) (itself SciSpace output) and does not change that document.

## How to read this review

- **Scope.** This review checks the citations and claims in `docs/AGIDesignChecklist.md` and suggests changes. It does not edit the checklist.
- **Two numbering schemes.** References from the checklist are written **C1, C2, … C30**, using the checklist's own numbers. Sources this review adds are written **[1], [2], …** and listed under [References](#references) at the end.
- **Evidence, interpretation and opinion.** Statements about what a paper reports are marked **Evidence**. Claude's reading of what that means for the checklist is marked **Interpretation**. Claude's design preferences are marked **Reviewer's opinion**.
- **How sources were checked.** Direct DOI resolution (doi.org, Crossref) was unavailable from the review environment. Each reference was checked against publisher pages, PubMed Central, author repositories, PhilPapers, Persée and similar indexes, found by web search. For most papers, support for a claim was judged from the abstract. For a few, a full-text summary was also available (Xu 2019, Rips 2017, Lidz & Gagliardi 2015). No paper was read in full, so a "Verified" rating means the claim is consistent with the abstract.

## Summary of findings

1. **All 17 cited works exist, and their DOIs match their titles.** No reference looks fabricated. Several are incomplete (missing venue, editors or subtitle), and one cites a French translation.
2. **Citations cover only about half the checklist.** Sections 3 (grounding), 4 (agency), 5 (memory) and 8 (evaluation) cite nothing, and neither do several specific items, such as self/world distinction and temporal representation.
3. **The citations nearly all come from one tradition**: developmental psychology and comparative cognition, mostly from the core-knowledge and rational-constructivist schools. The checklist cites no machine-learning, AI-safety or neuroscience-of-learning work, and no source that disagrees with its central premise.
4. **Several design opinions are stated as facts**, most importantly that AGI "would need" innate inductive structure. This is a live debate. Since 2023, results with generic networks trained on a child's-eye data [6, 7] and meta-learned compositionality [8] have reopened it.
5. **Important concerns are missing or thin**: alignment and safety, interpretability, energy efficiency as a design constraint, loss of plasticity in continual learning, and a developmental schedule (how and in what order the "starting kit" is trained).
6. **Many items cannot be tested as written.** Section 8 is closest to a test suite, but it has no benchmarks, thresholds or controls for training-data contamination.

**Structural citation issues:**

- **Gaps in numbering.** The checklist's numbers skip C3, C8, C10–C12, C16, C19, C20, C23–C25, C27 and C29 because it reuses numbers from `InnateCognitionReport.md`. CLAUDE.md allows this, but the checklist should say so in one line, or readers will think references are missing.
- **Uncited sections.** Sections 3, 4, 5 and 8 cite nothing. Nor do the items for temporal representation, self/world distinction, causal models, intrinsic motivation, catastrophic forgetting, episodic and semantic memory, or uncertainty estimation. Section 3 below suggests sources for these.
- **Uneven evidence.** Most sources come from one research programme (Spelke, Xu, Tomasello and colleagues). That is a strong programme, but its critics and the machine-learning evidence are absent.

## 2. Claim-by-claim check

Tags: **[Overstated]**, **[Contested]**, **[Outdated]**, **[Opinion as fact]**.

### Core principle

- *"AGI should not be defined as a larger pattern predictor alone. A human-like general system would need inductive structure, grounded models, active learning, flexible reasoning, and self-monitoring."* **[Opinion as fact] [Contested]**
  - Evidence: Lake et al. [3] make this argument at length. Botvinick et al. [2] answered that structure should be *learned* as far as possible rather than built in. Sutton's "Bitter Lesson" [1] argues that general methods that scale with compute have historically beaten hand-built knowledge. Mitchell & Krakauer [10] survey the unresolved debate over whether large language models "understand".
  - Interpretation: "would need" states as fact what is a research bet. "We bet that…" or "We hypothesise that…" would match the evidence.
- *"The human brain appears to begin with evolved predispositions rather than fully formed adult concepts."* **Reasonably supported** (C4, C14, C17), and suitably hedged with "appears".
  - The infant evidence behind some core-knowledge claims is weaker than this sentence suggests. Kulke et al. [4] failed to replicate three of four anticipatory-looking false-belief paradigms, and the fourth stopped replicating once confounds were removed.
- *"the design target is an effective developmental starting kit"* **[Opinion as fact]** This is a reasonable design stance, and C2 (Rips) itself argues that a starting kit is not enough.

### 1. Innate or built-in representational primitives

- **Numerical representation** ("approximate magnitude … before relying on language") **[Contested]**. Leibovich et al. [5] argue that infants and animals may track continuous magnitudes (area, density) rather than number, and that an innate number sense may not exist.
- **Agent and psychological representations** **[Contested]**. Early belief-tracking findings in infants have replication problems [4].
- *"These should function as priors or architectural biases, not as rigid rules."* **[Opinion as fact]** Reasonable, but untested for AGI. **[Outdated]**: since 2023, generic self-supervised networks trained on egocentric video from one child have learned object categories [6] and word–referent mappings [7] *without* object or agent priors. Both studies are limited in scale: they used only part of one child's experience, and they test perception and word learning, not reasoning. Interpretation: these results don't refute the case for priors, but the checklist should say the case is now contested and empirical.
- **Temporal representation** and **Self/world distinction** have no supporting citation. C5 covers time in animals and could be cited for the temporal item. No cited source covers the self/world item.

### 2. Learning and abstraction

- *"abstraction is plausibly innate as a set of biases and proto-conceptual mechanisms"* This is well hedged and matches C9 and C26.
- *"mature symbolic abstraction is developed through experience, language, and culture"* **[Overstated]**. One of the sources cited for it, C28, doubts that language alone explains how productive human thought is.
- *"The checklist should therefore require both preconfigured structure and learning-driven refinement."* **[Opinion as fact] [Outdated]**
  - Lake & Baroni [8] showed that a standard transformer trained by meta-learning on a stream of compositional tasks reached human-like systematic generalization, with no built-in symbolic machinery. Webb, Holyoak & Lu [9] reported analogical reasoning emerging in a large language model, though that claim has been disputed.
  - Interpretation: "preconfigured structure" might come from meta-learning or training curricula rather than hand-coding. The checklist should allow either route.

### 3. Grounding and world modeling

- No citations. Lake et al. [3] (intuitive physics and psychology, causal models) and Schölkopf et al. [21] (causal representation learning) would support the causal-model and physics items.
- **Acceptance test** ("solve novel problems after a small number of grounded demonstrations, not only after exposure to many linguistic descriptions") **[Contested]**. The idea that grounding is *necessary* is debated [10]. Humans also learn a great deal from language. The test is also hard to apply as written; Section 3.3 suggests a measurable version.
- **Psychological models** overlaps with §6 Perspective taking. Since 2023, LLMs have matched or beaten humans on several theory-of-mind tests while failing others [11], and the authors stress that this does not show the models have a theory of mind. The item should say how it will be tested so that it can't be passed by surface pattern-matching.

### 4. Agency, exploration, and action

- No citations. Possible sources: Oudeyer & Kaplan [18] for intrinsic motivation, Gopnik [19] on childhood as an exploration phase, Smith & Gasser [20] for embodiment.
- **Embodied or action-grounded interaction.** The "or" usefully avoids requiring a physical body. Whether physical embodiment is necessary is contested. Smith & Gasser [20] argue it is central to how intelligence develops.
- **Online adaptation** ("without catastrophically overwriting") **[Outdated]**. It covers only catastrophic forgetting [15]. Dohare et al. [16] showed a second failure: deep networks trained continually lose the ability to *learn new things* (loss of plasticity). The item should cover both stability and plasticity.
- *"A system that only generates plausible descriptions but cannot test, revise, and operationalize its models should not be considered fully general."* **[Opinion as fact]** This is a definition of "general", not a finding. Keep it, labelled as a definition.

### 5. Memory and learning across time

- No citations. Complementary learning systems theory [17] supports separate episodic and semantic memory and consolidation between them. The memory section could rest on it.
- **Consolidation and forgetting** overlaps with §4 Online adaptation and §8 "Learn continuously without losing prior capabilities".
- **Provenance tracking** is a strong item with no direct counterpart in the cited literature. Reviewer's opinion: keep it and link it to §7 Error monitoring.

### 6. Social and cultural intelligence

- *"Shared intentionality: Represent cooperative goals and norms that are not reducible to individual rewards."* **[Contested]** "Not reducible to individual rewards" is a theoretical position associated with Tomasello (C1). It is not a settled finding, and accounts based on individual reward and reputation compete with it. The item should name it as one hypothesis.
- *"social cognition may be a capability multiplier"*. Suitably hedged. C21 shows early human social-cognitive advantages, but no cited source tests the multiplier claim.
- **Norm and value learning** overlaps with §7 Goal and value stability and Safe social behavior. Together these are the checklist's only alignment content (see Section 3.1).

### 7. Metacognition and reliability

- **Uncertainty estimation**: *"Distinguish confidence from correctness."* As worded this is unclear. The intended property is presumably *calibration*: confidence should track how often the system is actually right. Kadavath et al. [14] measure exactly this in language models.
- **Reflective revision** **[Outdated] (understated difficulty)**. Huang et al. [12] found that LLMs often cannot correct their own reasoning without external feedback, and sometimes get worse when they try. Turpin et al. [13] showed that chain-of-thought explanations can misreport why a model gave an answer. Interpretation: self-reports are not reliable evidence of self-monitoring, so the item needs external checks such as interpretability (see Section 3.1).
- *"Early metacognitive capacities may provide developmental foundations…"* is supported by C15.

### 8. Evaluation criteria for "true" generality

- **"True" generality** (in the heading) is a loaded phrase. Morris et al. [30] propose grading AGI by performance, generality and autonomy instead of a yes/no test.
- **No benchmarks or thresholds.** None of the eleven tests names a task, a measure or a pass level. Chollet [28] defines intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency and proposes ARC as a measure. The ARC Prize 2024 report [29] shows that state-of-the-art methods reached 55.5% on the private set, below the 85% prize threshold. That is the kind of concrete target this section lacks.
- *"Develop new abstractions that were not explicitly listed in training data"* cannot be checked for systems trained on web-scale data without a contamination control (held-out, newly generated tasks).

### Bottom line

- *"The strongest design lesson … build the right inductive biases, then let them develop…"* **[Opinion as fact]** It reads as the conclusion of the evidence. It is one reasonable synthesis, and the opposing view [1, 2] should be mentioned.
- *"The exact combination remains an empirical research question"* is well put, and is the right note to end on.

## 3. Suggested improvements

### 3.1 Missing capabilities and design concerns

| Proposed addition | Why (evidence) | Suggested checklist wording |
|---|---|---|
| **Alignment and value learning** (new section, or §7 expanded) | Gabriel [22] separates aligning with instructions, intentions, interests and values. The checklist's "Norm and value learning" item covers only the descriptive part. | "Specify *what* the system aligns to (instructions, intentions, interests, values) and how conflicts between stakeholders are resolved." |
| **Safety under learning and exploration** | Amodei et al. [23] set out five concrete problems: negative side effects, reward hacking, scalable oversight, safe exploration and distribution shift. Intrinsic motivation (§4) makes safe exploration more pressing. | "Exploration is bounded by safety constraints; the system avoids reward hacking and degrades gracefully under distribution shift." |
| **Interpretability / inspectability** | Self-reports can be unfaithful [13]. Mechanistic interpretability [24] offers external ways to check what a system represents. | "Internal states relevant to goals, beliefs and uncertainty can be inspected by external tools, not only through the system's own explanations." |
| **Energy and compute efficiency as a design constraint** | The brain runs on roughly 20 W (estimates vary) [25]. Training large models has substantial energy costs [26]. §4 "Resource awareness" covers run-time awareness, not design efficiency. | "State an energy/compute budget for learning and inference, and report sample efficiency and energy use alongside accuracy." |
| **Continual learning: plasticity as well as stability** | Catastrophic forgetting [15] and loss of plasticity [16] are separate failures. | "Retains old skills *and* keeps the ability to learn new ones over a long task sequence." |
| **Developmental schedule / curriculum** | A "starting kit" implies a development process. Gopnik [19] argues that a protected period of broad exploration before exploitation is part of what makes human learning work. | "Specify the learning schedule: what is learned first, in what environment, with what exploration–exploitation balance." |
| **Embodiment (clarified)** | Smith & Gasser [20] argue for embodied development. The checklist's "embodied *or* action-grounded" leaves the requirement undecided. | Decide and state whether physical embodiment is required or whether simulated or tool-based action is enough, and give the reason. |
| **Intrinsic motivation (sourced and specified)** | Oudeyer & Kaplan [18] give a typology: knowledge-based (prediction error, novelty) and competence-based drives. | "Specify the intrinsic-motivation signal (e.g. learning progress) and show it improves exploration over an extrinsic-reward baseline." |
| **Consciousness / moral-status stance** | Butlin et al. [31] and Seth [32] reach different conclusions on whether computational AI could be conscious. The checklist is silent. | Reviewer's opinion: add a short scope note saying whether consciousness is a target, a non-target or an open risk (see Section 3.4). |

### 3.2 Overlaps to merge, and items to split

| Items | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| §4 Online adaptation, §5 Consolidation and forgetting, §8 "Learn continuously without losing prior capabilities" | **Merge** into one continual-learning requirement in §5, and keep one matching test in §8. |
| §3 Psychological models, §6 Perspective taking, §6 Joint attention | **Merge** the first two (both are theory of mind). Keep Joint attention separate, because it is interactive, not representational. |
| §6 Norm and value learning, §7 Goal and value stability, §7 Safe social behavior | **Move** into a new alignment and safety section (Section 3.1). |
| §3 Counterfactual simulation and §3 Causal models | **Merge**, or define counterfactual simulation as the most demanding level of causal reasoning, above association and intervention. |
| §7 Error monitoring ("contradictions, failed plans, hallucinations, and model mismatch") | **Split**: internal consistency checking vs. checking against external evidence. These need different mechanisms [12]. |
| §1 Self/world distinction | **Split**: bodily/agentive self (what my actions cause) vs. epistemic self (what I know), which overlaps with §7 Self-modeling. |

### 3.3 Making vague items testable

The protocols below are suggestions, not established standards, and the thresholds are placeholders to be set.

| Item | Concrete, measurable version |
|---|---|
| §3 Acceptance test ("solve novel problems after a small number of grounded demonstrations") | On procedurally generated tasks not seen in training (ARC-style [28, 29], or new physical puzzles in simulation), reach ≥ X% accuracy from ≤ N demonstrations, compared with human participants given the same demonstrations. |
| §2 Compositionality ("genuinely novel concepts") | Systematic-generalization splits in which test combinations of known primitives never appear in training, compared with human performance on the same splits [8]. |
| §3 Causal models | Predict the results of interventions on held-out causal graphs, scored separately for observational, interventional and counterfactual queries [21]. |
| §4 Online adaptation / §8 continual learning | On a long task sequence: average accuracy on earlier tasks (forgetting) *and* learning speed on each new task compared with a freshly initialized network (plasticity) [15, 16]. |
| §4 Intrinsic motivation | State coverage, or time to first reward, in sparse-reward environments compared with an extrinsic-only baseline [18]. |
| §6 Pragmatic language / Perspective taking | A battery of theory-of-mind tasks that includes perturbed variants, so success cannot rest on surface cues [11]. |
| §7 Uncertainty estimation | Expected calibration error, plus selective-prediction accuracy at fixed coverage, on in-distribution and shifted data [14]. |
| §7 Reflective revision | Accuracy change after self-revision *without* external feedback; it must not decrease [12]. |
| §8 "Know when it is uncertain or wrong" | Same as §7 Uncertainty estimation. Merge rather than test twice. |
| §8 "Develop new abstractions not explicitly listed in training data" | New tasks created after the training cutoff, with a documented contamination check. |
| §8 overall | Report a level on a performance × generality grid [30] rather than a pass/fail "true generality". |

### 3.4 Relation to biological computationalism

`docs/MilinkovicAruBiologicalComputationalismSummary.md` summarizes Milinkovic & Aru [33], who argue that biological computation is hybrid (discrete and continuous), scale-inseparable, and metabolically grounded, and that these features may matter for **consciousness**.

**Interpretation:**

1. **The paper is about consciousness, not general intelligence.** The checklist is about intelligence. Biological computationalism does not claim that intelligence requires a biological substrate. The two documents answer different questions, and the checklist should say which question it is answering.
2. **Shared ground: metabolic constraint.** Milinkovic & Aru treat the brain's energy budget as a "constitutive force" that shapes its computation. The checklist mentions energy only as run-time "resource awareness" (§4). Even if one rejects their consciousness claims, the point that an energy budget shapes a system's design carries over. This supports adding the energy-efficiency item in Section 3.1 [25, 26].
3. **Substrate as a design variable.** The checklist assumes, without saying so, that the architecture is independent of its hardware. If one takes biological computationalism seriously, hardware choices such as neuromorphic or analog substrates [27] become design decisions, not implementation details. A brief "substrate assumptions" note would make this explicit.
4. **Cross-scale dynamics vs. modular checklists.** The checklist lists separate modules (memory, metacognition, world model). Milinkovic & Aru's "scale-inseparability" suggests that, in brains, these may not split cleanly. This is speculative for intelligence, but it is a reason to test modules together, not only one at a time.
5. **Consciousness and moral status.** If a design followed the checklist *and* moved toward brain-like substrates, the risk of building something with morally relevant experience would go up on Milinkovic & Aru's view, and on Seth's [32]. On the computational-functionalist view used by Butlin et al. [31], the substrate matters less than the architecture. The checklist should take an explicit position, or at least flag this.

**Contested status.** Biological computationalism is a recent theoretical proposal with no direct experimental test. Computational functionalism, which it opposes, remains the working assumption of much consciousness-in-AI research [31].

## 4. Prioritized recommendations

1. **Mark design opinions as opinions.** Rephrase the core principle and the bottom line as stated hypotheses ("We bet that…"). Add one sentence giving the opposing scaling-and-learning view [1, 2].
2. **Add an alignment, safety and interpretability section.** Merge §6 Norm and value learning and §7 Goal and value stability and Safe social behavior into it, and add the problems from [22, 23, 24].
3. **Make §8 testable.** Give each test a benchmark or protocol, a comparison with humans, a threshold and a contamination control (Section 3.3). Consider replacing "true generality" with levels [30].
4. **Cite the uncited sections and diversify sources.** Add sources to §§3, 4, 5 and 8 (e.g. [3, 15–21]). Include at least one critic of core knowledge [4, 5] and recent machine-learning evidence [6–8].
5. **Update the "innate priors" claims** in §1 and §2 to reflect evidence since 2023 that generic or meta-learned networks acquire some of this structure from data [6, 7, 8]. Present the question as open.
6. **Merge the three continual-learning items** and add loss of plasticity [16] alongside catastrophic forgetting [15].
7. **Fix reference details.** Add the venue and pages to C13 and C28, the subtitle to C6, and the book and editors to C7. Note that C4 is a French translation. Add one line explaining the reuse of numbers from `InnateCognitionReport.md`. Also correct the "Social Science Research Network" venue for ref. 13 in `InnateCognitionReport.md`.
8. **Add an energy and compute efficiency requirement** and a short note on substrate assumptions, linking to the biological-computationalism summary (Section 3.4).
9. **Add a developmental-schedule item.** If the target is a "starting kit", say how the kit is trained: the curriculum, the environment and the exploration phase [19].
10. **Add a scope note on consciousness and moral status**, saying whether the checklist targets, avoids or is agnostic about machine consciousness [31, 32, 33].
